**Total Trials:** 490 across 10 blocks

## Key Findings

### Overall Accuracy by Condition
- **Target:** 47.5% (170 trials)
- **Filler-Old:** 96.3% (136 trials) 
- **Filler-New:** 3.3% (184 trials)

### Pre vs Post-Disambiguation Performance

**TARGET WORDS:**
- Pre-disambiguation: 1.2% (80 trials)
- Post-disambiguation: 93.8% (80 trials)
- **Improvement: +92.5 percentage points**

**FILLER-OLD (Previously seen):**
- Pre-disambiguation: 94.1% (68 trials)
- Post-disambiguation: 98.5% (68 trials)
- Improvement: +4.4 percentage points

## Interpretation

Strong evidence of **predictive coding** in auditory processing:
- Exposure to the disambiguated stimulus dramatically improves recognition of previously ambiguous targets
- The effect size (+92.5pp) is larger than the visual task results
- Consistent performance across 10 blocks (25-56% per block accuracy)
- Filler-new control condition shows appropriate near-zero response, confirming task validity

## Comparison to Visual Task
The auditory modality shows a stronger predictive coding effect compared to the visual Mooney task, suggesting that sound disambiguation may engage stronger predictive mechanisms.
